## Who buys and what they fear
A first-time government-job aspirant in Telangana/AP or anywhere in India, studying on a phone, often alone, often in Telugu or Hindi. Their fears: "Where do I start?", "Am I studying the right things?", "Am I on track?", "I failed a mock, should I quit?". The kit must answer each one on one screen.

## What every kit contains (sheets, in order)
1. **Start**: exam date → days left, today's 3 tasks (auto from the Plan), streak, % of syllabus done, next mock date. One glance = "am I on track?".
2. **Plan**: auto day-by-day calendar from today to exam day in 3 phases: *learn* (~50%), *practise* (~30%), *revise + full mocks* (~20%); one mock day a week, rising to two near the exam; one buffer day a week so missing a day never breaks the plan.
3. **Syllabus**: every topic from the official syllabus, with how often it was asked in past papers (high/med/low from PYQ analysis where available), confidence 1–5, done tick, spaced-revision dates (1-3-7-21 days, already built), and two links: **best free video** and **free notes / past questions**.
4. **Resources**: official links (notification, syllabus PDF, previous papers, answer keys, official mock test if the body offers one), free mock-test sites, the best YouTube channels per subject (English + Telugu/Hindi where relevant), standard books (named, no piracy links), open question banks.
5. **Mocks + mistakes**: score log with trend, and a mistake log split into *silly / didn't know / ran out of time*, so each mock tells you what to fix.
6. **Daily log**: minutes studied, topic, mood 1–5; streak and 7-day study days (built).
7. **Exam day**: admit card, ID, documents, travel check, the night-before and morning routine, time plan per section, "skip and come back" rule.
8. **Mind**: one-page playbook: 2-minute start rule, if-then plans, what to do after a bad mock, sleep, phone rules, a 60-second calm-down for exam nerves.

## Psychology used (stable research, named so buyers trust it)
- **Retrieval practice** beats re-reading (Roediger & Karpicke 2006) → mocks, PYQs and self-quizzing are the core.
- **Spacing** (Cepeda et al. 2006) → the 1-3-7-21 revision dates.
- **Interleaving** (Rohrer & Taylor 2007) → mixed-topic practice sets in the practise phase.
- **Implementation intentions** (Gollwitzer 1999) → "If it's 7 pm, then I do 2 topics" lines on Start.
- **Visible progress / goal gradient** (Kivetz et al. 2006) → % bars, streaks, days left.
- **Reappraising nerves as energy** (Jamieson et al. 2010) → the exam-day calm-down.
- **Fresh start effect** (Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014) → "Monday reset" when a week goes badly.

## Where links come from (rules)
- Official first: the exam body's own site (e.g. ssc.gov.in, ibps.in, tspsc.gov.in, psc.ap.gov.in, ctet.nic.in, nta.ac.in, upsc.gov.in, rrb sites).
- Free mocks: official mock if offered; otherwise well-known free mock pages (marked "free tier; sign-up may be needed").
- YouTube: channels chosen by size, recency (uploaded in the last 90 days) and language; one best video per topic, preferring full topic lessons over shorts.
- GitHub / Hugging Face: only open-licence question banks (licence recorded); used for a practice tab, never copyrighted papers.
- Never: pirated PDFs, Telegram piracy groups, paid-only links presented as free, scraped personal data.
- Every link stored with title, source, type and the date it was checked; a weekly GitHub Action checks every link (YouTube via its oEmbed endpoint) and the kit tests fail if any link is broken.
