# A281: Alpha Brain, a machine-learning stock picker the way quant funds build one (14 Nov 2026)

Akhil: "make a world-class quantitative trading brain: all markets and charts, fundamental and technical,
the best trading methods, at least 25 strategies, that makes money."

Script `research/a281_alpha_brain.py` (tests `tests/test_alpha_brain.py`, 3 pass). Results `a281_results.csv`,
`a281_ridge_weights.csv`. Trials T0031 (4 settings declared before the run) and T0032 (risk filter, added after).

## What world-class quant work looks like (checked 14 Nov 2026)
- Microsoft's **Qlib** (open source, MIT) is the public reference: 158 price/volume inputs ("Alpha158"), a
  gradient-boosted tree model (LightGBM) that ranks every stock each day, retrained on a schedule. On China's top 300
  stocks its published LightGBM result is about **11.4% a year above the index** (rank IC 0.049).
- **Renaissance Medallion**, the best record known: about 66% a year before fees, 39% after (1988–2018, Cornell 2019),
  from roughly 150,000 trades a day, thousands of long and short positions, and decades of private data.
- Common thread: many weak signals combined by a model, judged only on years it never saw. No single rule.

## What the Alpha Brain does
- **Universe:** 47 Nifty 50 stocks + 5 ETFs (Nifty, Bank Nifty, Next 50, gold, Nasdaq 100). Daily, 2009–2026.
- **38 inputs per asset, every week:**
  - 25 strategy signals as inputs: momentum over 6 windows, 12-1 momentum, distance from 4 moving averages,
    50/200 cross, Turtle 20/55, RSI 2/14, Bollinger, 52-week high, drawdown, MACD, reversal, up-day share,
    trend strength, 21-day range position.
  - Risk: volatility 21/63 days, downside volatility, beta to Nifty.
  - Market and macro: Nifty trend and momentum, **India VIX (fear)**, **USD/INR (rupee)**, **CPI inflation** (lagged 2
    months), breadth.
  - Fundamentals: none in the backtest. No free point-in-time history of Indian company results exists, and using
    today's numbers on old prices would be cheating. Noted, not faked.
- **Model:** gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn, the LightGBM algorithm family) averaged with a ridge model. Inputs
  ranked across assets each week (Qlib's method). Target: which assets do best over the next 21 trading days.
  Retrained every January on all earlier years, with a 21-day gap so no answer leaks in.
- **Trading:** ₹10,000 account share by share, every NSE charge, ₹15.34 DP per sell, 0.1% slippage, idle cash 4.7%.

- Rank IC (how well it ranks) is 0.030, about 60% of Qlib's China figure. It was positive in 6 of 8 years.
- Monthly: the brain beats the current bot by about 2% a year, but its worst fall is twice as deep.
- Weekly: charges wipe it out. ₹2,000 positions pay ₹15.34 per sell (0.8%) about 170 times a year.
- The plain 12-1 momentum rule did best, but that exact setting was **not** the one A273 chose on 2011–18 data (that one
  made 13.1% with a −35.5% fall). Picking it now would be hindsight.
- The model leaned most on: price above the 200-day average, Bollinger position, beta, MACD, 12-1 momentum (+), and
  against assets with a fresh 50/200 cross or a deep 63-day drawdown (−).

## Verdict
The world-class method, built and tested honestly on India data, gives **12.7% to 16.9% a year** on ₹10,000, with
worse falls than the current bot. It does not turn ₹10,000 into "anything". Medallion's edge came from scale, private
data and thousands of trades a day that ₹10,000 can't pay the charges for. Not a replacement yet. The brain
runs as a **paper track** (`paper/alpha-brain/`), and its monthly picks are logged so it builds a real record.
Live trading needs LIVE_TRADING=YES, GO + GATE_PASS files, 90 paper days and Akhil's "go".
